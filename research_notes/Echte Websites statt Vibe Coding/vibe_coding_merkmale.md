# Visual and code-level tells of "vibe-coded" / AI-generated websites (2025–2026)

Scope note: Almost all sources are practitioner blogs, community skill files, HN threads and design-media pieces from Aug 2025 – Oct 2026. There is one quantitative measurement (Adrian Krebs, 1,590 Show HN sites, Apr 2026), one conceptual academic paper (UW/Microsoft Research, Mar 2026) and one NN/g usability study on AI images (Aug 2026). Pattern lists are largely anecdotal but strongly convergent across independent authors.

## Which specific visual/code patterns are repeatedly named?

### Takeaway
The same cluster is named again and again: purple/indigo-to-blue gradients (often on white or on dark mode with glow), Inter (or the "next" defaults Geist / Space Grotesk / Instrument Serif), a centered hero with pill badge + two buttons, three (or six) identical icon-topped feature cards, unmodified shadcn/ui, glassmorphism, uniform radii/padding, coloured left/top accent stripes on cards, stat banners, numbered 1-2-3 steps, all-caps section labels, emoji icons, and identical fade-in on every section. No single pattern proves AI; the **combination without a reason** is the tell.

### Cited Findings

**Measured prevalence (the only quantitative source)**
- Adrian Krebs loaded 1,590 recent Show HN sites with Playwright and ran deterministic DOM/computed-style checks (no LLM judging, ~5–10 % false positives by manual QA). Result: 22 % "high" (4+ patterns), 32 % "medium" (2–3), 46 % "low" (0–1). Pattern groups: Fonts (Inter for everything, esp. centered hero headlines; Space Grotesk / Instrument Serif / Geist pairings; a serif italic accent word inside an Inter hero), Colours ("VibeCode Purple", permanent dark mode with grey body text, low body contrast in dark themes, gradients everywhere, large coloured glows/box-shadows), Layout (centered hero in generic sans, badge above H1, coloured top/left card borders, identical icon-topped feature cards, numbered "1, 2, 3" steps, stat banner rows, emoji icons in nav/sidebar, all-caps headings), CSS (shadcn/ui, glassmorphism). Dated 20 Apr 2026. Author's verdict: "Not really [bad], just uninspired" and "There is a difference between trying to craft your own design and just shipping with whatever defaults the LLMs output." — [Adrian Krebs, "Design slop"](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)
- GIGAZINE's summary of Krebs (23 Apr 2026, based on an earlier 500-site count) gives per-pattern shares: gradients 28 %; shadcn/ui unmodified 23.5 %; centered title 23.5 %; always-dark mode 20.3 %; templated feature grid (3 or 6 identical cards, icon on top) 20.1 %; glass panels with background blur 17.1 %; specific fonts (Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif, Geist, Syne, Fraunces) 15.8 %; coloured accent stripe on top/left of cards 13 %; statistics banner ("Over 10,000 users · 99.9% uptime") 12.2 %; purple CTA buttons 10.7 %; all-caps section labels/nav 10.5 %; numbered procedure sequence 9.4 %; capsule-shaped badge above title 4.7 %; vivid box-shadow glow 4.3 %; emoji icons in nav/sidebar 3.8 %. Note: GIGAZINE's numbers (500 sites, 21/46/33 %) do not match Krebs' final post (1,590 sites, 22/32/46 %) — likely an earlier version; treat individual percentages as indicative. — [GIGAZINE](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260423-design-slop/)

**Colour**
- Anthropic's own frontend-design guidance calls the generic look "distributional convergence" / "AI slop" and names "Clichéd color schemes (particularly purple gradients on white backgrounds)" and fonts "Inter, Roboto, Arial, Open Sans, Lato" plus system fonts; notes models still converge on Space Grotesk even when told to vary. Alternative: "Dominant colors with sharp accents outperform timid, evenly-distributed palettes"; commit to a cohesive theme via CSS variables; atmosphere via layered gradients/patterns instead of flat white. (12 Nov 2025) — [Anthropic/Claude blog](https://www.claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)
- Origin story widely repeated: Tailwind co-founder Adam Wathan's Aug 2025 X post apologising that he made every Tailwind UI button `bg-indigo-500` five years earlier, which is said to have seeded indigo into training data. Causal claim is commentary, not proven. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p); [AIbase news](https://news.aibase.com/news/20365)
- "Purple-to-indigo gradients have become the Times New Roman of AI-generated design." Fix: derive colour from the product's values and audience, not category trends. Also: neon palettes "five or six competing for attention"; "dark background plus glow equals premium"; multicoloured side tabs on every block ("Four competing accent colors don't add up to four highlights") — fix: treat colour as a shared page resource; status dots with no defined meaning. (Jeff Humble, Apr 2026) — [The Fountain Institute](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui)
- "A purple-to-blue gradient in the hero" used as decoration without meaning; fix: semantic colour system named by function (primary action, warning, success) instead of "gradient-start/gradient-end". (Kosta Canatselis, 23 Jul 2026) — [Spot the Slop](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)
- Accessibility angle: white text on Tailwind indigo-500 reaches 4.47:1 (misses WCAG AA 4.5:1); cyan-500 only 2.43:1. — [Dr. Web, Michael Dobler, 5 Oct 2026](https://www.drweb.de/ki-slop-design-vibecoding/)
- Other named colour tells: navy-blue-with-gradient schemes, black backgrounds with green/orange gradients, "dark glows on text", "1px borders paired with large shadow blur" (HN users gkolli, Wpnx330). — [Ask HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49290499)

**Typography**
- "Inter paired with a system fallback and nothing else" signals no typeface choice was made; fix: one display face with personality + one readable body face, "Change the font first." — [Spot the Slop](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)
- Lovable, v0 and Claude Code "all ship with the same defaults — Inter for type, ShadCN for components, Lucide for icons, Tailwind for spacing" (search-result summary of several posts). — [DEV Community, alanwest](https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh)
- The "second-generation" defaults that appear when models are told to avoid Inter: Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif, Geist, Syne, Fraunces; italic-serif accent word in an Inter headline; numbers in monospace. — [Adrian Krebs](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/); [GIGAZINE](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260423-design-slop/); [hn.nuxt.dev discussion summary](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/47864393)
- Ban-list items: Inter as both body and display; Title Case everywhere; all-caps/widely tracked body text; uniform font weights; display copy wrapping 5–6 lines; "Headlines that read like AI prompts". — [Laith0003 Anti-AI-slop ban list](https://github.com/Laith0003/ux-skill/wiki/Anti-AI-slop-ban-list)

**Layout and components**
- Gradient hero: "Purple to blue, centred headline, two buttons, a soft glow"; "Three feature cards with three icons and three sentences of equal length"; "the same three sections in the same order". Fix: lead with a real screenshot/product loop; show uneven features; break the symmetry once (bento with one large cell, off-centre hero). (21 Aug 2026) — [21st.dev](https://21st.dev/blog/website-not-look-ai-generated)
- "a gradient or blob background, and a single centered call-to-action button"; "a three or four item grid with an icon, a bolded word, and one sentence of description"; "the same rounded-corner card layout everyone else gets"; "a single safe sans-serif with no personality". (23 Oct 2026) — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/avoid-vibecoded-landing-pages)
- "Cards with the same corner radius everywhere… Same padding, same radius, same card height across the whole screen" → nothing stands out; fix: make primary elements larger/heavier, let secondary recede. Also "That damn left-edge coloured line + curve on a panel!" — [Spot the Slop](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)
- "Most content does not need a box around it" (cards nested in cards); fix: group with whitespace, proximity, typography; reserve cards for independently actionable items. — [The Fountain Institute](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui)
- Ban list layout items: three equal cards → asymmetric/bento; centered hero stack → left-aligned editorial or 60/40 split; `h-screen` mobile heroes → 70–80vh so content peeks; identical CTA pairs → one dominant primary; uniform section padding; glassmorphism "at most one glass element per page"; "Glow on everything → reserve glow for one accent"; emoji as decoration → one SVG icon library at one stroke weight. "Meta-fingerprint": "Purple gradient + Inter + centered hero + three cards." — [Laith0003 ban list](https://github.com/Laith0003/ux-skill/wiki/Anti-AI-slop-ban-list)
- Maya Brennan (UX Collective, 29 Sep 2026, as summarised by Dr. Web) counts 18 patterns, incl. gradient buttons with glow, emojis as icons, neon frames on dark backgrounds, glass look, heavy shadows, cards around every element, decorative colour orbs/blobs, unchanged chart-library colours, and excessive density (too much text and too many metrics in too little space). Dr. Web: AI slop = "Gestaltungsmuster, die einzeln sinnvoll sind, aber ohne Anlass kombiniert auftreten". — [Dr. Web](https://www.drweb.de/ki-slop-design-vibecoding/)
- Claude Code design guidance (as reported by Dr. Web) lists "identical cards with the same grey shadow and fade-in animations on every section" as traits to check the plan against. — [Dr. Web](https://www.drweb.de/ki-slop-design-vibecoding/)
- AIDesigner describes the typical vibe-coded output as "a purple gradient hero, three feature cards with rounded icons, and a testimonial section"; Velox Themes: "the same fonts, the same glowing cards, the same little badges… visitors spot it in a second." — [aidesigner.ai](https://www.aidesigner.ai/blog/vibe-code-website); [Velox Themes](https://veloxthemes.com/blog/how-to-vibe-code-a-website)

**Animation**
- "Either nothing moves, or everything fades in with the same timing." Fix: micro-interactions on primary buttons and inputs first; scroll animation only where it aids navigation/storytelling. — [Spot the Slop](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)
- Ban list: fade-up on every element → highlight one or two; 600 ms routine animations → 100–150 ms routine / 200–300 ms significant; linear easing; multiple parallax; endlessly animating elements; animated rainbow borders/glow rings. — [Laith0003 ban list](https://github.com/Laith0003/ux-skill/wiki/Anti-AI-slop-ban-list)
- Anthropic recommends one orchestrated page-load with staggered reveals rather than scattered micro-interactions. — [Anthropic/Claude blog](https://www.claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)

**Why it happens**
- Academic framing: vibe coding produces "a convergence toward the mean, dominant aesthetic"; "the push for frictionless generation can exacerbate homogenization"; users accept defaults because of "the cognitive and technical cost of iterating against the model's default bias". Illustrative default for a São Paulo café: "minimalist layouts, muted tones, sans-serif fonts"; generic "minimalist whitespace, standard sans-serif typography, and predictable hero images". Conceptual paper, no measured attribute frequencies. (Shin et al., UW / Microsoft Research, arXiv 2603.13036, 13 Mar 2026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13036v1)
- HN user Wpnx330: LLMs "reason" themselves into the same safe choices, so sites converge even when told otherwise. HN user ra0x3 calls it "a new 'Bootstrap' way of doing things". — [Ask HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49290499)
- Vague prompts don't fix it: Brennan's "Make it look less vibecoded" changed little; explicit decisions (one specific font, six hex values, flat colours, no emoji, no shadows) did. "Ein Prompt ersetzt keine Gestaltungsentscheidung, er überlässt sie den Voreinstellungen anderer Leute." (Markus Seyfferth) — [Dr. Web](https://www.drweb.de/ki-slop-design-vibecoding/)
- Warning that anti-slop fixes often just swap one default for another ("the failure mode of every anti-slop effort is replacing one default look with another"). — [search summary of DEV/21st posts](https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh)

### Inferences
- For a German small-business demo generator, the highest-weight visual tells to block are: purple/indigo/violet or blue-cyan gradients (esp. gradient text), Inter/Geist/Space Grotesk/Instrument Serif as defaults, centered hero + pill badge + two equal buttons, 3/6 equal icon cards, glass panels, glows, uniform radius everywhere, coloured left/top card stripes, stat banners with round numbers, numbered 1-2-3 steps used as decoration, all-caps eyebrow labels on every section, emoji icons, fade-up on every section.
- Because tells are combinatorial, a QA check could score "number of patterns present" like Krebs (≥4 = high) rather than banning single elements outright.
- "Swapping to the next default" (e.g. Inter → Space Grotesk/Fraunces, purple → teal glow) is itself recognisable in 2026; choices need a reason tied to the trade (e.g. trade-specific palette, typeface fitting the business's character).

### Gaps
- No source quantified "grid-line / dot-grid backgrounds", "star rows" or "Trusted by" logo strips specifically; they appear only implicitly ("logos of companies who are not customers", 21st.dev). Krebs' and Brennan's full lists were not fully retrievable (Brennan's UX Collective original was not found/accessible).
- No measured data on how ordinary small-business customers (as opposed to designers/HN readers) perceive these patterns.

## Which copy patterns read as AI?

### Takeaway
Named copy tells: category-level, interchangeable headlines ("Transform your workflow…", "Build the future of…", "Your all-in-one platform"), a subheadline that restates the headline more softly, filler verbs (empower, leverage, unlock), triplets ("fast, efficient, and reliable"), round-number stats, generic mission statements, throat-clearing intros, everything framed positively without trade-offs, and em dashes (contested). The cure everyone names is specificity: real numbers, names, dates and a real author's voice.

### Cited Findings
- "Transform your workflow with powerful, intuitive tools" is the "single strongest tell"; fix: "one specific claim that could only describe your product". "Generated pages have no author, and it shows." — [21st.dev](https://21st.dev/blog/website-not-look-ai-generated)
- "Build the future of work," "Scale without limits," "Your all-in-one platform" are averages of many headlines; fix: write until the founder would plausibly say it aloud. — [Spot the Slop](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)
- Subheadline "restating the headline in softer words"; generic value props that could describe any competitor; fix: "actual customer language instead of generic value props". — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/avoid-vibecoded-landing-pages)
- Ban-list copy items: placeholder names ("Jane Doe"), round-number stats, fake brand names, filler verbs ("empower," "leverage"), generic mission statements, slogans without context, vague testimonials, numbered lists without real order, throat-clearing intros, emoji as decoration; meta-fingerprint "'Empower, leverage, unlock' copy" and "Round numbers paired with generic mission statements"; principle "Voice over sterile politeness". — [Laith0003 ban list](https://github.com/Laith0003/ux-skill/wiki/Anti-AI-slop-ban-list)
- Stat banner "Over 10,000 users · 99.9% uptime" appeared on 12.2 % of measured Show HN sites. — [GIGAZINE](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260423-design-slop/)
- Em dashes "throughout the content" named as "biggest tell" by HN user wbnns. — [Ask HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49290499)
- Em dash evidence is contested: deleting dashes without fixing rhythm leaves text sounding machine-written; some argue it's a scapegoat. — [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/chatgpt-hypen-em-dash-ai-writing-1235314945/); [Plagiarism Today, Jun 2025](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/06/26/em-dashes-hyphens-and-spotting-ai-writing/); [Salt Agency](https://salt.agency/blog/in-defence-of-the-em-dash-what-the-ai-writing-debate-gets-wrong/)
- "AI adores lists of three, with examples like 'fast, efficient, and reliable'"; one strong word often better than three weak ones. — [DEV Community, raxxostudios](https://dev.to/raxxostudios/why-your-ai-generated-content-looks-like-ai-and-how-to-fix-it-4ao6)
- Tidy symmetry, missing trade-offs, and "everything is presented as positive" read as AI; let emphasis drive structure. — [WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/content-sounds-like-ai)
- HN user roundabout-host: odd descriptions of obvious UI elements (leftover notes to the developer). — [Ask HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49290499)

### Inferences
- German equivalents (my inference, not sourced): "Ihr Partner für …", "Qualität, die überzeugt", "mit Leidenschaft und Präzision", triplets like "schnell, ehrlich, fair" / "zuverlässig, kompetent, persönlich", headline + softer restating subline, "Willkommen bei …", superlatives without proof ("die beste …"), round stats ("100 % Zufriedenheit", "über 500 zufriedene Kunden"), and Gedankenstrich-heavy rhythm ("– schnell, ehrlich, fair"). These mirror the English tells one-to-one and also conflict with § 5 UWG when unbelegt.
- A demo text generator should prefer concrete, checkable facts (Straße/Stadtteil, Öffnungszeiten, real services from public data, Inhabername if public) over adjectives, and avoid three-adjective lists and dash-separated taglines.

### Gaps
- No German-language source specifically listing German AI copy tells for websites was found (t3n results were teaser/paywalled). The "not just X but Y" construction was not documented as a website tell in sources found.

## What do critics say is missing (photography, facts, editorial layouts, restraint, hierarchy, content-first)?

### Takeaway
Critics consistently say vibe-coded pages lack an author and specifics (names, numbers, dates, real screenshots/photos), hierarchy (everything same size/weight/radius), restraint (too many effects at once), asymmetry/editorial composition, and unhappy-path/interaction states. Real imagery of the actual business is the strongest anti-generic signal, though NN/g found unlabeled AI stock-like images did not reduce trust per se.

### Cited Findings
- Missing: "No numbers, names, screenshots, or dates"; stock content: "Generic illustrations, a smiling team photo that is not the team, logos of companies who are not customers"; fix: founder's name, real photo or signed note. — [21st.dev](https://21st.dev/blog/website-not-look-ai-generated)
- Principles: "Specificity over genericity", "Restraint over decoration" (2–4 effects per page), "Asymmetry over symmetry", "Editorial over decorative", "Contextual over abstract" (products in use, not floating on gradients), "Real names over placeholders", "Calm motion over default motion". — [Laith0003 ban list](https://github.com/Laith0003/ux-skill/wiki/Anti-AI-slop-ban-list)
- Hierarchy: uniform spacing/radii make the page flat; vary deliberately. Missing states: AI builds the happy path and skips empty/loading/error states "where trust is built or lost". Progressive disclosure instead of everything visible. — [Spot the Slop](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)
- Fountain Institute: one dominant colour, one accent, one neutral; depth through typography, contrast and surface levels instead of glow. — [The Fountain Institute](https://www.thefountaininstitute.com/blog/signs-vibe-coded-ui)
- Anthropic: extreme weight contrasts (100/200 vs 800/900), size jumps of 3×+, pairing serif with geometric sans; themed/contextual aesthetics. — [Anthropic/Claude blog](https://www.claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)
- Cultural flattening: model defaults (minimalism) override locally typical design conventions (e.g. information-dense Japanese web design). — [arXiv 2603.13036](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13036v1)
- NN/g study (77 US adults, fictional consulting firm, 6 hero images): "we found no evidence that the AI-generated images we tested reduced trust"; but when participants suspected AI they rated the site less favourably; warns of errors in text, hands, screens, reflections, background details; cited research (Schilke & Reimann, 13 experiments) shows disclosing AI use reduces trust. (Rachel Banawa, 21 Aug 2026) — [NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/ai-generated-images)
- Vendor/agency claims (unverified, interested parties): a real photo of the person doing the work is a major trust factor for local businesses; reserve real photos for team, premises, contact/about pages. — [Cube Creative](https://cubecreative.design/blog/partners/why-stock-photos-hurt-local-business-trust); [WebZum](https://webzum.com/blog/real-photos-beat-ai-images); [Jezweb](https://jezweb.com/blog/ai-imagery-honest-case)

### Inferences
- For demos of businesses where we may not use their own photos, the "missing specifics" gap must be closed with facts (address, Stadtteil, hours, actual service list, Google rating only where allowed) and honest placeholders, not fake team photos or fake testimonials — fake ones are both an AI tell and legally risky.
- Content-first: section order and emphasis should follow what the trade's customers need (e.g. Speisekarte/Öffnungszeiten for Gastro, Notdienst/Einsatzgebiet for Handwerk) instead of the universal hero → 3 features → steps → testimonials → CTA band sequence.

### Gaps
- No rigorous study on conversion impact of "vibe-coded look" vs. bespoke design for small-business sites. Evidence on real vs. AI/stock photography for local businesses is mostly vendor marketing; NN/g is the only controlled study and used a consulting-firm scenario.

## Are there articles, threads, galleries showing examples / naming the "shadcn/Tailwind default look"?

### Takeaway
Yes: Krebs' Show HN measurement (with shadcn-unmodified as a named check), HN parodies ("every vibe-coded website is the same page with different words", "Every AI-Coded Website Ever"), the Ask HN "how to spot vibe-coded sites" thread, Tailwind's indigo-500 story, and several open-source "anti-slop" skill/linters (Impeccable, Taste Skill, unslop-ui, Laith0003 ban list). The "default stack" is consistently named as Tailwind + shadcn/ui + Inter + Lucide.

### Cited Findings
- shadcn/ui "used in its default state, unmodified" detected on 23.5 % of measured Show HN sites. — [GIGAZINE](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260423-design-slop/); method: [Adrian Krebs](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)

### Inferences
- Krebs' method (Playwright + computed-style checks, count of patterns per page) is directly reusable as an automated QA gate for generated demos: e.g. detect gradient backgrounds/text, `backdrop-filter: blur`, font-family Inter/Geist/Space Grotesk, ≥3 sibling cards with identical size + icon on top, border-left/top accent colour on cards, all-caps letter-spaced labels, emoji characters in headings/nav, identical scroll animations on all sections.

### Gaps
- No curated side-by-side gallery (AI default vs. human redesign) for small-business/local sites was found; examples are mostly SaaS/Show HN landing pages. Reddit (r/webdev, r/web_design) and Lovable/v0 community threads were not reached within the search budget.
