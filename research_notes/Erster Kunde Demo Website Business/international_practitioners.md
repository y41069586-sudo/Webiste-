# International practitioners: time to first paying client with "build the demo website first, then pitch it" (as of 14 Nov 2026)

**Method and limits of this research.** Reddit (r/webdev, r/freelance, r/Entrepreneur and others) **could not be reached**. The search tool returns "domains not accessible to our user agent: reddit.com", and direct fetches were blocked as well. Indie Hackers posts returned **HTTP 403**, so for those posts only the search-engine snippets were available, not the full text. X/Twitter and YouTube transcripts were not readable either. The notes below therefore rest on a small number of fully read primary sources (Substack, DEV Community, the Website Profit Course blog, Skool) plus snippets. **Every number is labelled with its source type.** Where it says "snippet", the number was not checked against the full original.

Legend: **[EH]** = first-hand practitioner report · **[VK]** = vendor or course seller with an interest in selling (a tool, course, lead list or community) · **[SN]** = from search snippet only, original not read in full.

---

## 1. Time from the start of outreach to the first paying client (with the number of demos and contacts)

### Takeaway
There are hardly any well-documented first-hand reports with a measured timeline from first contact to first payment. The few concrete stories show either very fast closes in small numbers (11 contacts in one week led to 5 sales; 43 emails over about 3 weeks led to 1 paying client), or no customer yet after an unknown number of messages. The often-quoted "30–45 days to your first client" comes from vendors and is not measured.

### Cited Findings
- **Amber (vibecodewithamber), Substack, 10 Oct 2026 [EH, but with tool recommendation]:** She built a free mock-up site for 11 local businesses (no website or a broken one, found on Google Maps). Channel: phone call to get the email address, then a link by email. Result: "I contacted 11 businesses that week", 5 sales, "45% close rate", "$5,000", "about $1,000 each on average". All 11 mock-ups together took "under two hours". She gives no separate time-to-first-sale figure. Monthly maintenance was offered, amount not stated. She recommends the AI builder CatDoes with no affiliation disclosed, and she also mentions about "$900" in spending in September. No course is sold (free newsletter). → [Substack](https://vibecodewithamber.substack.com/p/how-i-made-5000-in-a-week-selling)
- **Indie Hackers, "I sent 43 cold emails with my own tool. 17 replied. 1 paid." [EH + VK (founder promotes his own tool), SN]:** 43 emails over about 3 weeks (5–7 per day, personal Gmail), 17 replies, 1 paying client. The post was not readable in full (403), so the price, date and whether the sites were pre-built demos are unconfirmed. → [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-sent-43-cold-emails-with-my-own-tool-17-replied-1-paid-here-s-the-unofficial-launch-329774e623)
- **Ray DelVecchio, Website Profit Course blog, 31 Mar 2021 [EH, author runs a training site for web designers = VK context]:** His first paid project was a roofer. First contact came through a **referral** ("my dad did business with him in the past"). Site "built in advance to show him during that meeting" (30-minute meeting), "He paid me $500 that day", one long night of work, "about $100/hour". The roofer later became a monthly client. Not cold outreach, so only partly comparable. → [Goodreads mirror of the blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/21141626-what-i-learned-making-100-hour-on-my-first-project)
- **"Harun" (koda2026), DEV Community, 6 Sep (year missing, account created Aug 2026) [EH, unverified]:** Messages to local businesses (electrical shops, car washes, CCTV companies, painters). "Most of the messages weren't even seen." One demo built specifically for a CCTV company, one reply ("💐"). Status: "I haven't got a client yet." No number of contacts, no time span. A search engine summary claimed this person "landed a first client", **which the original contradicts**. → [DEV](https://dev.to/koda2026/i-tried-selling-a-website-to-a-local-business-at-12-heres-what-happened-56h2)
- **"First paying or testimonial client within 30 to 45 days with consistent effort", roughly 100–200 quality emails at a 3–8% positive reply rate [VK, lead-tool vendor]** → [B2BLeadFinder](https://b2bleadfinder.io/blog/how-to-get-first-10-web-design-clients)
- **"Most designers land their first local client within a week of running a targeted Maps campaign" [VK, lead-tool vendor, SN]** → [Leadlu](https://www.leadlu.com/blog/businesses-without-websites)
- **Example calculation with "10% reply rate, 25% close rate" giving about 1 client per week [VK, assumption, not measured]** → [LeadsAgent](https://leadsagent.io/blog/web-agency/cold-outreach-scripts-web-design-clients)

### Inferences
- The two most concrete first-hand reports (11 → 5 and 43 → 1) imply between about 1 and 45 contacts per close. Both rely on **personal contact via phone or Gmail** and very small numbers. They are not robust rates.
- Vendor figures (30–45 days, 100–200 emails) are of the same order as the 43-email case but are not backed by data.
- No source documents the case of a postal letter as the first contact (see section 3).

### Gaps
- Reddit threads (the main source of first-hand reports with "x demos, y replies, z weeks") were not reachable. That is the biggest gap.
- No source gives a clean start date of outreach plus the date of the first payment.
- The full Indie Hackers posts (price, date, demo yes/no) could not be read.

## 2. Reply rates and close rates for pre-built demo outreach, and how many give up

### Takeaway
Reported reply rates range from 0.37% (generic cold email to small businesses, without a demo) to 40% (43 emails, 17 replies). The high figures come almost entirely from founders who sell their own outreach or demo tool. Close rates from replies are rarely reported. Systematic data on drop-outs does not exist, only individual "no client yet" posts.

### Cited Findings
- **"Reply rates went from 2% to 25%" through a live demo site in the cold email [VK: founder of a tool that generates demo sites automatically, SN, post about 8 months old as of the search]** → [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-a-tool-that-generates-a-live-website-for-a-local-business-before-you-contact-them-qoZTIOY6f8DC83FRfxmP)
- **43 emails → 17 replies (about 40%) → 1 paying client (about 6% of replies, 2.3% of contacts) [EH + VK, SN]** → [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-sent-43-cold-emails-with-my-own-tool-17-replied-1-paid-here-s-the-unofficial-launch-329774e623)
- **Counterexample: "820 emails to small businesses → 3 replies (0.37%)"** (audit-style cold email, no pre-built site). The author also found when checking by hand that few websites had problems an owner would care about, and that many addresses were guessed (info@, hello@) [EH, SN] → [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/800-cold-emails-later-heres-what-actually-moves-the-needle-and-what-s-a-complete-waste-of-time-1e67d7e295)
- **11 contacts (phone, then email with mock-up) → 5 sales = 45% close rate [EH]** → [Substack](https://vibecodewithamber.substack.com/p/how-i-made-5000-in-a-week-selling)
- **Generic cold email baselines (not demo-specific):** Smartlead 3.43% average reply rate [VK] → [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/cold-outreach-the-ultimate-guide); Hunter 2025 4.5% average reply rate, 5.6% vs 3.6% with two personalisations, 4.4% with tracking vs 7.4% without [VK, but large data set] → [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email)
- **Vendor claim, "no website" niche: 10–20% reply rate for personalised campaigns, restaurants 12–18% vs 3–5% with generic templates [VK]** → [B2BLeadFinder](https://b2bleadfinder.io/blog/cold-email-to-business-without-website)
- **Older HN discussions on cold outreach in general: "1 meeting out of 50 emails", reply rate "in single digit percentage" [EH, older, not demo-specific, SN]** → [HN: Does cold outreach actually work?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16289763), [HN: Experiment: Cold emailing businesses](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=3649048)
- **Practitioner warning: "no website" is only a weak signal. Many such businesses are booked out through referrals, run on social media, or have no budget [EH opinion, author links to a lead platform = VK context, Jun (year unclear)]** → [DEV: The Web Design Client Trap](https://dev.to/webdevamin/the-web-design-client-trap-stop-chasing-businesses-without-websites-254l)
- **Spec work and free mock-ups are now offered as standard by many small agencies** ("free working demo … before you pay a dime"), so an unsolicited demo may stand out less than it used to [VK, agency pages] → [respecdigital.com](https://respecdigital.com/), [landonscales.com](https://www.landonscales.com/free-mockup), [crlocalbusiness.com](https://crlocalbusiness.com/free-website-mockup)

### Inferences
- The size of the gap between 0.37% (without a demo, mass email) and 25–40% (with a demo, hand-picked, few contacts) is plausible as a direction, but the high values come from small samples and from people who sell a demo tool. A realistic planning range for cold email with a demo is more likely in the low double digits for replies and low single digits for closes per contact. That is an inference, not measured.
- Survivorship: the only fully read "failure" report (Harun) has no numbers. Systematic drop-out rates could not be found.

### Gaps
- No source with a large sample (> 100 demos) and a documented close rate.
- No data on how many people try this model and stop without a client.

## 3. Which channel was fastest (walk-in, phone, email, DM, postal)?

### Takeaway
The only fully documented quick success (5 sales in one week) used a **phone call first**, then the demo link by email. Personal contact or a referral also dominated in the older example (meeting with a pre-built site). For **postal letters with a QR code to a pre-built demo**, no first-hand practitioner report could be found, only general direct-mail and QR-tracking material from vendors.

### Cited Findings
- **Phone → email with mock-up link, 11 contacts, 5 sales in one week [EH]**. She also suggests walk-ins, without saying she did them herself → [Substack](https://vibecodewithamber.substack.com/p/how-i-made-5000-in-a-week-selling)
- **Referral + in-person meeting with a pre-built site → payment the same day ($500) [EH, 2021]** → [Website Profit Course / Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/21141626-what-i-learned-making-100-hour-on-my-first-project)
- **Messages (channel unclear, probably WhatsApp or DM): "Most of the messages weren't even seen", no client [EH, unverified]** → [DEV](https://dev.to/koda2026/i-tried-selling-a-website-to-a-local-business-at-12-heres-what-happened-56h2)
- **Cold email via personal Gmail, 5–7 per day: 1 client after about 3 weeks [EH/VK, SN]** → [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-sent-43-cold-emails-with-my-own-tool-17-replied-1-paid-here-s-the-unofficial-launch-329774e623)
- **Postal mail with QR code:** the search found only vendor material (PostGrid, QRLynx, Lob and others) on QR tracking in direct mail, no web-designer case study. QRLynx points out explicitly that a scan proves neither delivery nor a sale [VK] → [QRLynx](https://qrlynx.com/blog/qr-code-direct-mail-guide), [PostGrid](https://www.postgrid.com/qr-code-marketing-in-direct-mail-advertising/)
- **Video "Building Websites without Permission" (Saiwok, YouTube):** a site built without being asked for a Vietnamese street-food restaurant. "Luckily the owner was cool with it", no sale documented [EH, SN] → [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1kZrM3WGhuw)

### Inferences
- In the reports that were found, channels with **synchronous personal contact** (phone, meeting) close faster than asynchronous ones (email, DM). This fits the advice found again and again that the demo works best when the owner "scrolls through their own site" ("Watching an owner scroll through their own salon on a real website is the whole pitch", Amber).
- For postal mail as the only channel there is **no international practitioner benchmark** in this research. The project's own measurements will be the first data.

### Gaps
- No first-hand report on postal letters or postcards with QR code to a pre-built demo website.
- No direct comparison of channels with the same lead quality.

## 4. Price points of the first sale and what made the first client say yes

### Takeaway
Documented first sales sit at **$500** (2021, referral) and about **$1,000 per site** (2026, phone plus mock-up), often with a monthly maintenance upsell. In both cases the reason for the yes was that the owner **saw a finished site for their own business** before deciding.

### Cited Findings
- **$500 one-time, paid the same day, later a monthly management contract [EH, 2021]** → [Website Profit Course / Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/21141626-what-i-learned-making-100-hour-on-my-first-project)
- **About $1,000 per site on average (5 sites = $5,000), maintenance offered [EH, Oct 2026]**. Reason given: "the mock did most of the work, since people were saying yes to something they'd already seen" → [Substack](https://vibecodewithamber.substack.com/p/how-i-made-5000-in-a-week-selling)
- **Typical market range for local-business websites "500 to 2,000 dollars" [opinion/VK]** → [DEV](https://dev.to/webdevamin/the-web-design-client-trap-stop-chasing-businesses-without-websites-254l) (according to search snippet); "$200–$300" or "$500+" as price suggestions in course and playbook offers [VK, Gumroad sales pages] → [Goodreads post](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/21642654-how-to-sell-websites-4-ways-to-add-value-for-local-web-design-clients)
- **Vendor advice on demo-first pitches: short email under 75 words, one specific observation, live demo link, a yes/no question [VK]** → [SkipThePitch](https://skipthepitch.app/resources/demo-first-web-design-outreach)
- **Argument against "no website" as the only criterion: a site sells when it solves a concrete problem (bookings, quote requests with photos, proof of work, clear packages) [opinion]** → [DEV](https://dev.to/webdevamin/the-web-design-client-trap-stop-chasing-businesses-without-websites-254l)

### Inferences
- The project's price of €597 sits in the range of the documented first sales ($500–$1,000). The upsell to monthly maintenance shows up in both first-hand sources, which supports a monthly model.
- The documented yes moments involved a **personal contact while the owner looked at the demo**. A letter cannot replicate that. A follow-up channel, or making contact easy (phone, WhatsApp), probably matters more here than in the documented cases. That is an inference.

### Gaps
- No documented first-sale price for an anonymous checkout without a conversation (the self-serve model of this project).

## 5. Survivorship bias: do success stories mostly come from course and tool sellers?

### Takeaway
Yes, clearly. Most of the sources found with concrete success numbers are vendors of lead tools, demo generators, courses, Gumroad templates or paid communities. Fully verifiable, neutral first-hand reports are rare. Of the fully read ones, one was a success with an unmentioned tool recommendation, one was a 2021 referral case, and one had no client yet.

### Cited Findings
- **Skool community "Learn AI" (Morningside Education):** a post on the "already done" method (build websites or chatbots for prospects in advance). The page itself has no first-hand numbers, only aggregate claims: "7,250+ agencies" trained, "1,000+ clients landed", "$18M+ in client revenue", community of more than 330,000 members [VK, sells the AAA Accelerator] → [Skool](https://www.skool.com/learn-ai/how-to-get-clients-already-done-method-build-websiteschatbots-etc-for-them-or-use-pre-built-for-their-niche)
- **YouTube Short "11 Million Businesses Have No Website": "a student of [the creator] sold …" (cut off) [VK, course marketing, SN]** → [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/lGVO8gzO7zw); similarly "This AI Finds Businesses With No Website… Then Builds it & Emails them the link" [VK/tool demo, SN] → [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gef_VQbBVmU)
- **Search results for "spec website / first client" were mostly Gumroad sales pages** (cold-email templates, "first client" guides) and vendor blogs (B2BLeadFinder, Leadlu, LeadsAgent, SkipThePitch, Weblessleads, NoSiteSearch, ProspectSignal) [VK] → e.g. [Leadlu](https://www.leadlu.com/blog/businesses-without-websites), [NoSiteSearch](https://www.nositesearch.com/blog/why-local-businesses-without-websites-are-your-best-outreach-leads)
- **The best numbers (2% → 25% reply rate; 17/43 replies) come from Indie Hackers founders who sell exactly that demo or outreach tool [VK, SN]** → [Indie Hackers (tool)](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-a-tool-that-generates-a-live-website-for-a-local-business-before-you-contact-them-qoZTIOY6f8DC83FRfxmP)
- **The Amber post (5 sales in one week) recommends a specific AI builder (CatDoes) with no disclosed relationship** → [Substack](https://vibecodewithamber.substack.com/p/how-i-made-5000-in-a-week-selling)

### Inferences
- Treat published success numbers as an **upper bound**. The real distribution (many people with 0 clients) is under-documented because failures are rarely written up with numbers.
- Since about 2025 there has been a visible wave of AI demo-generator tools that market exactly this method. This raises both the hype and the competition: local businesses increasingly receive unsolicited demo sites.

### Gaps
- No neutral survey on the success rate of people trying this model.
- Reddit, the most likely source of independent failure reports, was technically not reachable.
