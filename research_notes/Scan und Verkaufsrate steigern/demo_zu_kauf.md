# Demo-Besuch zu Kauf: was die Kaufrate nach dem Scan erhöht (SeitenWerk, Stand 20.11.2026)

Legend: [DE] = German data, [US] = US-only data, [HARD] = survey or experiment with a stated method and sample, [ANEC] = single practitioner anecdote, [VENDOR] = a seller of the tool or service reports it. Some sources were already gathered in earlier notes in this folder tree (`Nach dem Scan zum Kauf/`, `Erster Kunde Demo Website Business/`); they are re-cited here with their original URLs so these notes stand alone.

Overall caveat: **I found no published data at all on the demo-visit → purchase rate for demos sent by letter (QR code).** Every close-rate number below is either from email or phone outreach with a demo, or from general e-commerce or landing-page research. The numbers indicate direction only.

## 1. Case studies and benchmarks for "we already built your website" sales (spec-work outreach)

### Takeaway
The few reported close rates for build-first demos (up to 45%) all come from cases where the seller **talked to the owner** (phone first, or a reply conversation), and from very small samples or tool sellers. Purely written outreach without a conversation reports far lower rates (about 2% of contacts). There is no German case with numbers, and no case of a no-call checkout after a mailed demo.

### Cited Findings
- [US][ANEC] "Amber" (Substack, 10.10.2026): built mock-up sites for 11 local businesses (no website or a broken one, found via Google Maps). **She phoned each one first to get an email address**, then sent the demo link. 5 sales out of 11 contacts ("45% close rate"), about $1,000 each ($5,000 total). All 11 mock-ups took "under two hours". She recommends a specific AI builder without disclosing any relationship. — [vibecodewithamber.substack.com](https://vibecodewithamber.substack.com/p/how-i-made-5000-in-a-week-selling)
- [US][ANEC + VENDOR] An Indie Hackers founder sent 43 cold emails with a pre-built demo and got 17 replies (about 40%) but only 1 paying client (about 2.3% of contacts, 6% of replies). — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-sent-43-cold-emails-with-my-own-tool-17-replied-1-paid-here-s-the-unofficial-launch-329774e623)
- [US][VENDOR] The founder of claimsite.app (a tool that generates demo sites automatically) reports reply rates rising from **2% to 25%** after switching to a build-first cold email. The tool can also mail a postcard with a QR code to the demo for about $2, but the post gives **no scan or sales numbers for the postcards**. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-a-tool-that-generates-a-live-website-for-a-local-business-before-you-contact-them-qoZTIOY6f8DC83FRfxmP) (page returned 403 on direct fetch, so details come from the search summary and earlier notes)
- [US][ANEC] Counterexample with no demo: 820 audit-style cold emails to small businesses got 3 replies (0.37%). — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/800-cold-emails-later-heres-what-actually-moves-the-needle-and-what-s-a-complete-waste-of-time-1e67d7e295)
- [VENDOR] Example calculations used by agency-tool sellers assume a "10% reply rate, 25% close rate" or an "8% reply rate and a 35% close rate **on calls**". These are assumed figures, not measured ones. — [LeadsAgent](https://leadsagent.io/blog/web-agency/cold-outreach-scripts-web-design-clients); [Systemify](https://www.systemifyautomation.com/blog/why-cold-outreach-is-the-most-important-growth-channel-for-agencies-serving-local-businesses)
- [VENDOR] A 2026 sales-statistics roundup says only **0.2–2% of cold contacts** become a closed deal. — [Martal](https://martal.ca/sales-statistics-lb/)
- [US][ANEC, via search summary] A freelancer guide reports that clients who cannot pay a large bill at once responded well to "$0 down and $150/month", with a **six-month minimum** to protect against early cancellation. — [CodeStitch freelancing guide](https://codestitch.app/complete-guide-to-freelancing/) (not fetched in full)
- [US][VENDOR] US "$0 down" pay-monthly website offers are common, for example $0 down plus $33/month, with a $397 one-time alternative. These are sales pages and say nothing about conversion. — [RateGather](https://rategather.com/pay-monthly-websites/); explainer: [Hello Web Designs](https://www.hellowebdesigns.com/blog/what-does-a-0-down-website-actually-mean/)
- [DE] Response to cold advertising letters in general: a post mailing with 1–2% response counts as successful, and the craft trades tend to be lower. One summary cites a 0.5% average for new-customer letters (Deutsche Post Dialogmarketing-Monitor 2013; attribution uncertain). — [Handwerksblatt](https://handwerksblatt.de/betriebsfuehrung/erfolg-mit-werbebriefen); [handwerk magazin](https://www.handwerk-magazin.de/mailings-per-brief-zu-neuen-auftraegen-176428/)
- [DE][VENDOR study, existing customers only] In the CMC Print-Mailing-Studie 2026, **46% of orders arrived only from week 5 after dispatch** onwards (35 shops, 858,490 mailings, average CVR 4.0%). These were mailings to existing customers, not cold leads. — [Deutsche Post / CMC 2026, via earlier notes](https://www.horizont.net/marketing/nachrichten/cmc-print-mailing-studie-2025-print-mailings-wirken-auch-in-wirtschaftlich-schwierigen-zeiten-227589)

### Inferences
- The one high close rate (45%) depends on a **conversation before the demo**: a phone call made the owner expect the link. SeitenWerk's letter-only model with a no-call checkout is structurally closer to the "written outreach" cases (about 2% of contacts or less). On a few dozen letters, zero sales is statistically normal and not yet proof that the offer fails.
- Worked example (assumed, not measured): 40 letters × a 5% scan rate = 2 visits. Even an excellent 10–20% visit→purchase rate gives an expected 0.2–0.4 sales per batch. The bottleneck is **scans first**, and only then the demo→purchase step.
- The CMC long tail (half of orders after week 5) suggests a demo should stay reachable longer than a few weeks before a batch is judged a failure.

### Gaps
- No published case reports scan→purchase for **letter + QR + demo**. No German practitioner reports close rates for spec sites.
- Reddit and YouTube threads with concrete numbers (r/web_design, r/Entrepreneur, agency YouTubers) did not show up in searches. Reddit content appears to be poorly indexed by the search tool, so this is a search limitation, not proof of absence.

## 2. Pricing psychology: one-time vs subscription vs "€0 setup, monthly"; anchoring; risk reversal

### Takeaway
Strong experimental evidence shows that breaking a price into **smaller, more frequent amounts** raises sign-ups a lot (daily vs monthly framing: 29.9% vs 7.1%). Merchant surveys back installment options. Evidence on guarantees is consistent in direction but anecdotal in size. There is no robust German data on how Handwerker react to monthly fees, and craft firms report that **their own customers are increasingly price-sensitive** (87%).

### Cited Findings
- [US][HARD, field experiment, Acorns app, thousands of new users] Framing the same savings amount as **$5 per day** instead of **$150 per month** quadrupled enrolment: **29.9% daily vs 10.3% weekly vs 7.1% monthly**. After 3 months, 19% of the daily group were still enrolled vs 5% of the monthly group. Daily framing also removed the participation gap between lower and higher income groups. — [Hershfield, Shu & Benartzi 2018 (working paper PDF)](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Hershfield_Shu_Benartzi_2018_SSRN-id3097468.pdf)
- [US][HARD, lab] Gourville (1998), "Pennies-a-Day": restating a cost as a small daily amount ("temporal reframing") makes the transaction look better, because people compare it with small everyday expenses. — [Journal of Consumer Research 24(4), via IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v24y1998i4p395-408.html)
- [DE][survey of merchants, about 2020, B2C retail] Ibi research / TeamBank "Ratenkauf im deutschen Einzelhandel": 28% of merchants offer installment payment. Of those, **69% report higher conversion**, 74% report larger baskets and 64% report higher customer satisfaction. These are merchants' own perceptions, not measured lifts. — [TeamBank study PDF](https://www.teambank.de/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/studie_ratenkauf-im-deutschen-einzelhandel.pdf)
- [DE][HARD, Bitkom 2025, n=504 craft firms with at least 1 employee, phone survey KW 23–29 2025, ±4%] **87% of craft firms say their customers have become clearly more price-sensitive**, 74% say customers are more critical, and 89% say customers expect individual offers. — [Bitkom Studienbericht Digitalisierung des Handwerks 2025](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-01/bitkom-studienbericht-handwerk.pdf)
- [DE][older IDG survey, undated] German Mittelstand firms bought software mainly as licences (about 94%), with rental accepted mostly for non-core software. This is an older and imperfect proxy for an "own it vs rent it" preference. — [Computerwoche](https://www.computerwoche.de/article/2686728/computerwoche-studie-was-der-mittelstand-von-saas-haelt.html)
- [US][ANEC] A single-business test: adding a visible 30-day money-back guarantee raised sales by 21%. A 1-year guarantee instead of a 90-day one reportedly doubled conversion, with refunds rising only modestly. When a free trial was present, the guarantee made no difference. — [QuickSprout](https://www.quicksprout.com/what-converts-better-free-trial-versus-money-back-guarantee/)
- [VENDOR] Break-even logic for guarantees: the lift needed equals r/(1−r), where r is the refund rate. A 5% refund rate needs about a 5.3% conversion lift. The 10–30% lift ranges that vendors cite are not traceable to studies. — [Churntools](https://churntools.com/blog/should-you-offer-money-back-guarantee); [roast.page](https://roast.page/ask/do-money-back-guarantees-work)
- [US][qualitative, older] NN/g: business buyers rank pricing as their top information need, and hidden prices make companies look "evasive and untrustworthy". — [NN/g (via earlier notes)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-price/)
- [HARD, lab] IKEA effect: people who assembled a product themselves would pay a premium of about 63% for it. The effect disappears if the task is not completed. This is relevant to "set up first, then pay". — [Norton, Mochon & Ariely 2012, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1777100)
- [VENDOR] Typical US price points: small-business builds by freelancers cost roughly $1,000–6,000, plus $25–250/month for hosting and edits. A worked example shows the monthly route costing more over the first year ($3,600 vs $3,300). — [Siteseeker](https://siteseeker.ai/blog/how-much-to-charge-for-a-website)

### Inferences
- SeitenWerk's tariffs differ widely in first-year cost: Basis 597 € + 12×19 € = **825 €**, Care+ 597 € + 12×99 € = **1,785 €**, Komplett 12×179 € = **2,148 €**. "Komplett" removes the upfront barrier, but owners who do the arithmetic may read it as the most expensive option. It works as an anchor that makes Basis look cheap. Showing the yearly total next to each monthly price is the transparent choice, given NN/g's finding and the increasingly price-critical audience.
- The temporal-reframing evidence argues for **also** stating small units (Basis: about 0.63 €/day for running costs; Komplett: about 5.90 €/day) next to the legally required clear total. This is untested for B2B website offers and must never hide the total or the 12-month commitment (UWG transparency).
- "First payment only 7 days after go-live" plus the 14-day guarantee is strong risk reversal. It only works if it appears **above the fold on the demo and in the letter**, not just in the setup flow. The guarantee anecdotes show the effect depends on visibility.
- The IKEA effect supports the current flow (enter details or adapt the demo first, then pay), as long as the step is short enough to be finished. An abandoned setup brings no effect.

### Gaps
- No German study on Handwerker or beauty/gastro owners and website subscriptions vs one-time prices. No A/B data on "€0 setup + monthly" vs "one-time + monthly" for websites.
- Atlas & Bartels (2018, JCR) on periodic pricing and perceived contract benefits looks relevant but was not read: [PDF](https://home.uchicago.edu/~bartels/papers/Atlas-Bartels-2018-JCR.pdf).

## 3. Trust signals for German small business owners buying online from an unknown solo provider

### Takeaway
Distrust is a measurable but minority reason for abandoning a purchase (about 19% of US shoppers). The strongest documented credibility signals are a **visible real organisation and person** (address, phone, email, photos). Seals measurably help in German consumer e-commerce. Craft firms value online reviews highly (72%), which favours **showing their own Google reviews on their demo** over SeitenWerk's (non-existent) testimonials.

### Cited Findings
- [US][HARD, Baymard, n=1,026 US respondents, 2016] 19% had abandoned a checkout in the last 3 months because they did not trust the site with their card details. Baymard's seal tests found that even a made-up generic seal raised perceived security, and that perceived security of the payment fields matters more than actual security. — [Baymard: perceived security](https://baymard.com/blog/perceived-security-of-payment-form); [Baymard: site seals](https://baymard.com/blog/site-seal-trust)
- [US][HARD, Baymard 2026, average of 50 studies] Average cart abandonment is 70.22%. Reasons include extra costs too high (40%), card distrust (19%), forced account creation (18%), a checkout that was too long (17%) and a total cost that could not be calculated up front (12%). — [Baymard cart abandonment](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)
- [US][HARD, Stanford Web Credibility Project, 3 years, 4,500+ people] Guideline: "Show that there's a real organization behind your site". Secondary summaries name address, phone number and email as top credibility signals, followed by photos of the people. — [Stanford Credibility Guidelines](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines); [Userlike summary](https://userlike.com/blog/10-crucial-elements-website-credibility)
- [DE][HARD-ish, ECC Handel with Trusted Shops, BillSAFE, PayPal; >1,000 online shoppers; older study, year not confirmed] Shops with a quality seal reached up to 23.1% higher conversion. 74% of shoppers pay attention to seals, and 64% are more likely to buy in a certified shop. This is a sponsored B2C e-commerce study. — [etailment](https://www.etailment.de/magazin/ecc-guetesiegel-pushen-die-konversionsrate-in-onlineshops); [IFH Köln](https://www.ifhkoeln.de/sechs-von-zehn-kleinen-und-mittleren-online-shops-haben-guetesiegel/)
- [DE][HARD, Bitkom 2025, n=504] **72% of craft firms see online customer reviews as a big opportunity**. 88% use directory entries such as Google, and 40% are on review platforms. — [Bitkom 2025](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-01/bitkom-studienbericht-handwerk.pdf)
- [DE][VENDOR, agency self-study] Of 500 craft websites tested, 78% had no photo of the owner. The same agency claims click-to-call converts "10–15×" better than forms, which is unverified. — [Huchel Medienagentur](https://huchel-medienagentur.de/wissen/handwerker-website-worauf-es-ankommt)
- [DE][legal] Fake or bought reviews and invented references are unlawful under the UWG (§ 5, and UWG Annex No. 23b/23c since the 2022 Omnibus rules on reviews). This matches SeitenWerk's rules. — [Baymard is not the source; see IHK guidance referenced in earlier notes](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf) (general advertising-law guidance; Annex No. 23b/c not verified in this pass)

### Inferences
- Legal ways to make up for missing customer references:
  1. Founder visibility: real photo, name, own address, **landline or mobile number**, short personal message.
  2. Third-party proofs that exist now: Stripe as payment processor, SEPA mandate, the written 14-day guarantee, the "first payment after go-live" rule, an Impressum with a real address.
  3. The prospect's **own real Google reviews** quoted on the demo, which shows the work is about them. Check Google Maps terms and how quotes are attributed.
  4. Transparent price lists and annual totals (Baymard's "couldn't see total cost" reason).
  5. Once the first customers exist, real named references with permission.
- A Trusted Shops or similar seal is designed for shops and B2C. Its proven effect is B2C, and for a solo B2B service with few transactions, cost and benefit are unclear. It is low priority compared with a phone number and photo.

### Gaps
- No German study on what makes **business owners** (rather than consumers) trust an unknown online service provider. No data on how much an Impressum or phone number specifically changes conversion.

## 4. Friction: should a purchase require a call? Callback options; preferred channels of Handwerker

### Takeaway
All German craft firms use phone and email, and 62% use messengers such as WhatsApp. The one high-converting demo case relied on a phone conversation. Fast replies matter a lot when a lead makes contact (about 7× more likely to reach a meaningful conversation within the first hour, US data). Outbound calls to businesses after a scan are **legally risky** in Germany. An **opt-in "Rückruf anfordern" / phone number** combined with the no-call checkout looks like the best fit, but there is no direct A/B evidence for 500–2,000 € services.

### Cited Findings
- [DE][HARD, Bitkom 2025, n=504] **All** firms use email, smartphone and landline. 62% use messengers (WhatsApp/Telegram) often or occasionally, 46% use SMS and 36% use video calls. **77% still use letters**, and 26% still use fax. 48% offer online appointment booking, and 27% use online payment services. 58% say they do not know what is technically possible. — [Bitkom 2025](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-01/bitkom-studienbericht-handwerk.pdf)
- [US][HARD, HBR 2011, about 1.25 million leads, 2,241 US firms] Firms that tried to contact a web lead within an hour were nearly **7× more likely to qualify it** (reach a meaningful conversation with a decision-maker) than firms that waited longer. Only 37% replied within an hour, and about 23% never replied. — [Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" (BYU record)](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711)
- [DE][legal] Advertising calls to businesses need **presumed consent based on concrete circumstances that existed before the call**. Usefulness of the offer or common industry practice is not enough, and the recipient's later behaviour does not count (BGH I ZR 27/08; OLG Hamm 4 U 189/09). The DSK requires "a concrete reason from the called party's sphere of interest (e.g. a prior business contact)". — [IHK Arnsberg guidance](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf); [DSK Orientierungshilfe Direktwerbung 2022](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- [DE][legal] Advertising email or WhatsApp messages need prior express consent, also between businesses. A **single** unsolicited advertising email is already unlawful (BGH I ZR 218/07). — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- [DE][VENDOR blogs] German agency blogs recommend showing phone and WhatsApp prominently, because customers prefer different channels. One cites ">60% messenger usage" without a source. — [belumedia](https://www.belumedia.de/blog/website-handwerker); [handwerk-vom-fach](https://www.handwerk-vom-fach.de/mehr-anfragen-ueber-die-website-conversion-tipps-fuer-handwerker/)
- [US][VENDOR] Personalised video in cold outreach: Loom reports a +19% reply rate for Intercom, and Vidyard reports +216% for Terminus and +26% reply rates across Salesloft users. These are self-selected vendor cases and measure email replies, not purchases. — [Loom / Intercom](https://www.loom.com/customers/intercom); [Vidyard / Terminus](https://go.vidyard.com/customers/terminus-uses-video-boost-sales-response-rates-3x); [Salesloft](https://www.salesloft.com/resources/blog/salesloft-vidyard)

### Inferences
- Making a call **mandatory** adds friction for owners who want to decide alone (often outside working hours). Offering **no** human contact removes the channel that converted in the only high-close case. A middle path: keep the checkout open and add (a) a visible mobile number with WhatsApp "write us" (already planned), (b) a "Rückruf anfordern" button with a time window, which creates consent and allows a call, and (c) bookable 10-minute "Kurz besprechen" slots (the booking system from task #101). Measure who uses each.
- Speed matters: if a request or WhatsApp message comes in, answer within an hour during business hours. The HBR effect is US and B2B, but the mechanism (interest fades fast) likely transfers.
- Owners are on the phone or WhatsApp during the day and often read post or demos in the evening. An automatic reply ("Ich melde mich bis morgen 9 Uhr") plus a quick personal reply is realistic for a solo founder.
- No outbound calls based only on a scan event: a scan is behaviour after the advertising, which courts disregard for presumed consent (OLG Hamm).

### Gaps
- No German data comparing purchase rates with vs without a callback option for 500–2,000 € services. No representative data on whether Handwerker prefer WhatsApp or phone for **buying** (as opposed to talking with their own customers).

## 5. Demo page best practices: what to show first

### Takeaway
There is no controlled study of personalised spec demos. General landing-page data favours **short, simple language**, transparent prices and real-person signals. Practitioner and retargeting evidence suggests showing **their own** specifics (name, real reviews, what they viewed). A founder video is supported only by vendor data on outreach replies.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR, large dataset, Unbounce 2024: 41k+ landing pages, 464M visitors] Median landing-page conversion is 6.6%, and 6.1% for professional services. **83% of visits are on mobile**, and desktop converts about 8% better. Pages at a 5th–7th grade reading level convert at 11.1%, about 2× professional-level text (5.3%). Difficult words correlate −24.3% with conversion. — [Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/)
- [US][VENDOR] Direct-mail retargeting that **showed the exact item the person viewed** (car-dealer postcards) produced 7 sales, 4 of them for the exact vehicle shown (no control group). — [LeadPost](https://leadpost.com/direct-mail-retargeting/)
- [DE][VENDOR best case] Seventyseven/Styleboom: a letter sent when a cart was **not reactivated after 2 days** showed the abandoned items plus recommendations and an incentive. — [Deutsche Post best case](https://www.deutschepost.de/de/p/print-mailing/insights/best-cases/seventyseven.html)
- [DE][VENDOR study] CMC 2024: **time-limited vouchers clearly speed up sales**. CMC 2023: response amplifiers such as QR codes, vouchers and stickers raised CVR by up to 33% (existing customers). — [Horizont CMC 2024](https://www.horizont.net/marketing/nachrichten/cmc-print-mailing-studie-2024-wie-limitationen-die-performance-von-gutscheinen-beeinflussen-221678); [Deutsche Post CMC 2023](https://www.deutschepost.de/de/p/print-mailing/insights/studien/cmc-printmailing-studie-2023-bestandskunden.html)
- [DE][HARD, Bitkom 2025] 75% of craft firms see higher visibility to customers as a benefit of digitalisation, but only 32% (2022: 55%) see digitalisation as securing their existence. 58% do not know what is technically possible. — [Bitkom 2025](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-01/bitkom-studienbericht-handwerk.pdf)
- [DE][HARD, Bitkom 2025] **94% of craft firms (≥1 employee) already have their own website**, and 88% use directory entries. — [Bitkom 2025](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-01/bitkom-studienbericht-handwerk.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because 94% of craft firms already have a website, the 6% without one are a **self-selected minority**: often owners with full order books who see no need, or very small or late-career firms. The demo must answer "why do I need this at all?" with the owner's own concrete gain, such as fewer calls about opening hours, appointment requests at night (48% of firms already offer online booking), or being findable when people search the business name. It should not lean on generic "digital is the future" claims, which convince fewer owners each year (32%, down from 55%).
- What to show first on a mobile screen (83% mobile): (1) their own business name and city in the site itself, (2) their real Google rating or reviews if allowed, (3) one short sentence from the founder with photo ("Ich habe diese Seite für Sie gebaut. Sie gehört Ihnen ab …"), (4) price and risk reversal in one line ("ab 597 € + 19 €/Monat · erste Zahlung erst nach Go-live · 14 Tage Geld zurück"). Use simple language (Unbounce reading-level data).
- An honest expiry ("online bis {Datum}") is supported by the CMC limitation finding. It must be real (the demo really goes offline), otherwise it is misleading under § 5 UWG.
- A short founder video (30–60 s, filmed once and generic, or personalised per lead) is a plausible next step, but evidence exists only for **email reply rates** (vendor data), not for purchases.
- A "with vs without website" comparison is plausible but untested. Any numbers in it (e.g. "X% search online") must be backed by approved claims (CLAUDE.md L1 rule).

### Gaps
- No A/B data on personalised demos (own reviews vs none, founder video vs none, expiry vs none). SeitenWerk must test these itself. With very few visits, though, A/B tests will be underpowered for months, so qualitative signals (session recordings, if consent allows, or direct questions) matter more.

## 6. Follow-up after a scan without a purchase (German law)

### Takeaway
Letters remain the only safe channel to an owner who has not given consent. They rest on Art. 6(1)(f) DSGVO (direct marketing as a legitimate interest), with an Art. 21 objection right and Art. 14 notice. Email, WhatsApp, SMS and outbound calls need consent (or for calls, documented presumed consent beforehand). Retargeting cookies or pixels need consent under § 25 TDDDG. A **second letter that refers to the visit** is not addressed by any source found, so it needs a legal check. Evidence from retargeting by post (US/CA vendors) shows lifts of +34% to +43% against holdouts.

### Cited Findings
- [DE][legal] Personalised advertising letters can be based on Art. 6(1)(f) DSGVO. The OLG Stuttgart relied on Recital 47 ("direct marketing may be regarded as a legitimate interest"), and the AG Hamburg-St. Georg likewise found postal direct marketing compliant. A balancing of interests is still required, and an objection ends the advertising. — [IT-Recht Kanzlei on OLG Stuttgart](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/olg-stuttgart-briefwerbung-datenschutz.html); [Kanzlei Dr. Bahr](https://www.dr-bahr.com/news/wettbewerbsrecht/die-dsgvo-erlaubt-ausdruecklich-werbung-per-briefpost.html)
- [DE][legal, vendor blog] Advertising letters to a legal entity under its company name fall outside the DSGVO, while letters to named individuals (and sole traders) fall within it. An Art. 14 notice (including that the data comes from public sources) belongs in the first letter. — [briefkampagne.de](https://briefkampagne.de/wissen/dsgvo-b2b-direktwerbung/); [cortina-consult](https://cortina-consult.com/datenschutz/wissen/postwerbung/)
- [DE][legal] The DSK guidance on direct marketing (Feb 2022) applies the legitimate-interest test and requires a concrete prior reason for B2B calls. — [DSK OH Direktwerbung](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- [US][VENDOR] An insurer held back 65% of non-converting site visitors as a control and sent postcards to the rest: **+43% conversion**. Parachute/PebblePost reported +34% vs holdout, but the targets were look-alike prospects, not visitors. — [LeadPost / Reach Dynamics](https://leadpost.com/direct-mail-retargeting/); [casestudies.com Parachute](https://www.casestudies.com/company/pebblepost/case-study/parachute-boosts-conversion-rate-by-34-with-pebblepost)
- [CA][HARD-ish, Canada Post] Benefit Cosmetics: programmatic mail to past website visitors gave +37% sales of the promoted products vs control. — [Canada Post Incite 2024 (PDF)](https://origin-www.canadapost.ca/cpc/doc/en/n/incite-2024-issue-01-datagram.pdf)
- [Method] With small volumes, holdouts are underpowered: a 5% holdout on 1,000 pieces gives only 50 control records. — [directmail.io](https://directmail.io/blog/direct-mail-roi-holdout-cell-attribution-methodology)

### Inferences
- The scan event comes from a server-side redirect with a token printed on the letter. No cookie is set and nothing is read from the device, so it is **probably not** a § 25 TDDDG case. It is still personal data if the business is a sole trader, so it must be listed in the privacy notice (Art. 13/14) and covered by the legitimate-interest balancing. This is my inference, not a sourced legal opinion.
- A follow-up letter that says "Sie haben sich Ihre Seite angesehen" may feel intrusive and could tip the balancing test. A safer wording is neutral ("Ihre Seite ist noch bis {Datum} online. Ich habe sie inzwischen verbessert …"), sent to both scanners and non-scanners, or a short "hot lead" letter without naming the tracking. This needs legal review.
- The highest-value legal step after a scan is to **collect consent on the demo itself**: "Link per E-Mail/WhatsApp schicken", "Rückruf anfordern", or "An Ablauf erinnern" with a double opt-in. After that, one reminder email or a callback is allowed.

### Gaps
- No court decision or authority statement found on **letters triggered by a website visit** (postal retargeting) in Germany. No German data on response to a second letter after a demo visit. The Art. 21 objection and suppression list must be honoured in every follow-up.
