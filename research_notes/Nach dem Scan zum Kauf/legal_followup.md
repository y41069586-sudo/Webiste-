# Legal follow-up after a QR scan / demo visit (B2B, Germany, as of Nov 2026)

> Not legal advice. Research notes for the report writer. Primary sources checked: DSK Orientierungshilfe Direktwerbung (Feb 2022, full text), DSK OH Digitale Dienste v1.2 (Nov 2024, full text), IHK Arnsberg Merkblatt "Werbung per Telefon, Brief und E-Mail" (March 2022, full text). The statute text of § 7 UWG was read via a dejure.org summary because gesetze-im-internet.de returned HTTP 503. Court decisions are mostly cited through IHK, DSK or law-firm summaries. The full judgments were **not** read; this is marked where it matters.
>
> **Numbering note:** Since 28.05.2022 the current § 7 Abs. 2 UWG reads: Nr. 1 = phone calls (consumers: prior express consent; other market participants: at least presumed consent), Nr. 2 = automated calling machines, fax, "elektronische Post" (prior express consent), Nr. 3 = concealed sender identity or no opt-out address ([dejure.org § 7 UWG](https://dejure.org/gesetze/UWG/7.html)). The IHK Merkblatt dated March 2022 still uses the older numbering (phone = Nr. 2, e-mail = Nr. 3). This is an inference from comparing the two texts; check the official text before quoting paragraph numbers.

## 1. § 7 UWG: Does a scan or demo visit give consent for a phone call or e-mail? Is a follow-up letter allowed? Can the visit be mentioned in the letter?

### Takeaway
A QR scan or demo visit is **not** an express consent (needed for e-mail, SMS, WhatsApp and fax) and very probably **not** a presumed consent ("mutmaßliche Einwilligung") for a sales call. The BGH test asks whether the business, judged on the facts **before** the call, expected the call or would welcome it. Looking at a page that a letter pushed on the recipient is a weak fact, and no court has held that it is enough. A follow-up **postal letter** is in principle allowed unless the business has objected (§ 7 Abs. 1 S. 2 UWG, Art. 21 DSGVO). Mentioning the visit in that letter is not a UWG problem as such. It is a data-protection and "creepiness" risk (see Q2).

### Cited Findings
**Telephone (B2B, presumed consent)**
- For calls to businesses that are not consumers, presumed consent is enough. The test looks at the circumstances **before** the call and at the type and content of the advertising. A general connection to the called company's line of business is not enough: "Anderenfalls wäre Telefonwerbung gegenüber Gewerbetreibenden nahezu unbeschränkt zulässig" (BGH, 16.11.2006, I ZR 191/03). Usefulness of the offer is not enough either. — [IHK Arnsberg Merkblatt, März 2022](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- "Erforderlich ist, dass auf Grund konkreter tatsächlicher Umstände ein sachliches Interesse der anzurufenden Person an der Telefonwerbung vermutet werden kann. Maßgeblich ist, ob die werbende Person bei verständiger Würdigung der Umstände annehmen durfte, die anzurufende Person erwarte einen solchen Anruf oder werde ihm jedenfalls positiv gegenüberstehen" (BGH, 11.03.2010, I ZR 27/08). — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf); decision listing at [rewis.io I ZR 27/08](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/kd2-10-03-2010-i-zr-2708/)
- Later behaviour of the called person does not count. Presumed consent must exist beforehand (OLG Hamm, 25.02.2010, 4 U 189/09). — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- No presumed consent even for a "customer satisfaction" call that followed a prior action such as a postage refund (KG Berlin, 15.09.2021, 5 U 35/20). — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- Presumed consent must cover the **type** of advertising (the phone channel), not only its content (BGH, 20.09.2007, I ZR 88/05, "Suchmaschineneintrag"). A common acquisition practice in the industry does not create presumed consent (I ZR 27/08). — [rewis.io I ZR 88/05](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/l93-19-09-2007-i-zr-8805/), [rewis.io I ZR 27/08](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/kd2-10-03-2010-i-zr-2708/) (summarised via search; full text not read)
- The DSK applies the same test under DSGVO Art. 6(1)(f) for B2B calls: "Vielmehr ist es jedenfalls notwendig, dass für den Anruf ein konkreter und aus dem Interessenbereich des Anzurufenden herzuleitender Grund vorliegt (dies kann z. B. ein geschäftlicher Vorkontakt sein)." — [DSK OH Direktwerbung, Feb. 2022, Nr. 1.4.2 b](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- No presumed consent where there was no prior business relationship (OLG Schleswig, 20.06.2022, 6 U 29/22). — [rewis.io](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/exm-20-06-2022-6-u-2922/) (search summary only)
- Outlier: LG Kleve (2023) allowed a single advertising call to a business because the service was objectively useful. This sits uneasily with the BGH line, which says usefulness is not enough. — [IT-Recht Kanzlei (law-firm blog)](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/lg-kleve-telefonwerbung-mutmassliche-einwilligung.html)
- An express objection rules out presumed consent. LG Nürnberg-Fürth (2023, 19 O 1485/23) found unreasonable harassment through calls and e-mails made against a business's stated wish. — reported via search summary of [gesetze-bayern.de GRURRS 2023, 36137](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/Y-300-Z-GRURRS-B-2023-N-36137?hl=true) (not read in full)
- The caller ID must not be suppressed (§ 15 Abs. 2 TTDSG, now TDDDG). Fines up to €300,000. — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)

**E-mail / electronic messages**
- Advertising by "elektronische Post" requires prior express consent for consumers **and** businesses. The only exception is § 7 Abs. 3 UWG (existing customer whose address was obtained in connection with a sale, similar own products, no objection, opt-out notice at collection and with every use). — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf); [dejure.org § 7 UWG](https://dejure.org/gesetze/UWG/7.html)
- An e-mail address shown on a letterhead or business card is not consent, also between businesses (LG Baden-Baden, 18.01.2012, 5 O 100/11). A **single** advertising e-mail is already unlawful (BGH, 20.05.2009, I ZR 218/07). Opt-out does not create consent. — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- The advertiser must show and prove consent (OLG Hamburg, 29.07.2009, 5 U 226/08). — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- Even a feedback or rating request attached to a transactional e-mail is advertising and needs consent (BGH, 10.07.2018, VI ZR 225/17). — [dejure.org](https://dejure.org/2018,28246); [IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/kundenzufriedenheitsanfrage-rechnung-bgh.html) (full judgment not read)
- DSK: "Ohne das Vorliegen der Voraussetzungen des § 7 Abs. 3 UWG bedarf es für die Nutzung von E-Mail-Adressen zu Werbezwecken grundsätzlich einer Einwilligung." If a contact channel is not allowed under § 7 UWG, "fehlt es bereits an einem berechtigten Interesse" under DSGVO (citing OVG Saarland, 16.02.2021, 2 A 355/19). — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 1.4, 1.4.1](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)

**Postal letter**
- Advertising by letter "ist grundsätzlich zulässig". If the recipient of a personalised advertising letter asked the sender to stop, that wish must be respected (§ 7 Abs. 1 S. 2 UWG; LG Lüneburg, 04.11.2011, 4 S 44/11). A "Keine Werbung" sticker on the mailbox must be respected. — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- A letter is misleading or harassing if it pretends not to be advertising (e.g. a fake handwritten note from a friend) or carries false labels such as "Vertraulicher Inhalt" or "Eilige Terminsache" (KG Berlin, 19.06.2015, 5 U 7/14). — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- Robinson lists: checking them is voluntary and not required by law in Germany. The DSK recommends pointing objecting persons to them as an extra option. — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 5.1](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- DSGVO side: after an Art. 21 objection, data may be kept in an internal "Werbesperrdatei" (Art. 21 Abs. 3 with Art. 6(1)(f)). Objections must be implemented "unverzüglich". For letters already in production, an exception may apply in individual cases. — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 5.1, 5.4](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- Enforcement: since December 2021 only real competitors (active "in nicht unerheblichem Maße"), qualified associations, consumer bodies and chambers may send a warning (Abmahnung). The contractual penalty is usually several thousand euros. — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Phone after a scan: risky, leaning toward not allowed.** A scan is a reaction to our own unsolicited letter, not a request for contact. Under BGH I ZR 27/08, a scan might be argued to show "Interesse", but the courts stress a business pre-contact or a concrete reason "aus dem Interessenbereich des Anzurufenden" (DSK). A scan is passive and anonymous to the visitor, who did not ask to be called. A competitor or association could easily argue that the visitor only looked. I found no decision that treats a website visit or QR scan as presumed consent. Treat a phone call without an explicit callback request as an **Abmahnung risk**, which the project's own rules already exclude (CLAUDE.md: "keine Kaltanrufe").
- **E-mail, SMS, WhatsApp after a scan: forbidden** without express consent. The visitor gave no address. Even if their public business e-mail is known, using it for advertising is unlawful (one e-mail is enough).
- **Follow-up letter: allowed**, if (a) the business has not objected (the "Kein Interesse" button, an opt-out, a returned letter with a refusal), (b) there is no "Keine Werbung" mailbox sticker that we know of, and (c) the letter is clearly recognisable as advertising and repeats the Art. 21 objection notice in a visually separate block (DSK recommends this "bei jeder Werbesendung").
- **"Sie haben Ihren Entwurf angesehen" in letter 2:** UWG does not prohibit it in itself. It is a DSGVO question: is using scan behaviour to select and personalise letters within Art. 6(1)(f), or is it profiling that needs consent? See Q2. A neutral wording works for all recipients and avoids the issue ("Ihr Entwurf ist noch bis {Datum} online"). If the visit is mentioned, the processing must be disclosed in the first letter's Art. 14 notice.

### Gaps
- No court decision found on a phone call or e-mail **after a QR scan or PURL visit** from an advertising letter. The B2B presumed-consent case law concerns cold calls, directory entries and prior customer relationships.
- Full texts of I ZR 27/08, I ZR 88/05, I ZR 191/03 and LG Nürnberg-Fürth 19 O 1485/23 were not read. Quotes come from the IHK Merkblatt and database summaries.
- The official § 7 UWG text could not be fetched from gesetze-im-internet.de (HTTP 503); a dejure summary was used.

## 2. DSGVO / TDDDG: Is per-business scan tracking lawful? Retargeting? WhatsApp or SMS to the public phone number?

### Takeaway
Data about a **sole trader** (most Handwerksbetriebe: Einzelunternehmer, e.K.) is personal data. Logging "lead X scanned on date Y" through a token in the URL is most plausibly a DSGVO matter: Art. 6(1)(f), plus disclosure in the Art. 14 notice of the first letter and an Art. 21 objection notice. **§ 25 TDDDG consent** is triggered only if something is stored on or read from the device (cookies, local storage, fingerprinting, third-party pixels). The DSK treats deliberately opening a URL as using an "expressly wanted" service, but one Bavarian authority slide lists "Tracking-Links" under § 25, so this is a grey area. **Retargeting** (uploading lead lists to Meta or Google Custom Audiences, or setting ad pixels on the demo) needs **consent**. **WhatsApp or SMS** to a public number is "elektronische Post" and needs **prior express consent**, so it is forbidden here.

### Cited Findings
**Personal data of sole traders**
- The DSGVO does not protect data of legal persons. General company contact data (info@firma.de) is usually not personal data (Recital 14). For sole traders and craftspeople, details can often be traced to a natural person, so Art. 13/14 duties apply. — [Brandi Newsletter 03/2019 (law firm)](https://www.brandi.net/fileadmin/redakteure/Newsletter/pdf/archiv/Newsletter_0319.pdf); see also [Luther, KUR 07/08 2019 (law firm)](https://luther-services.einmahl.de/uploads/tx_fwluther/KUR_07_08_19_Beitrag_Rabe.pdf)

**Legal basis, Art. 14, Art. 21**
- Direct marketing can rest on Art. 6(1)(f) (Recital 47). The balancing must be done case by case and must consider "die vernünftigen Erwartungen der betroffenen Person". Expectations "können dabei nicht durch die nach der DS-GVO vorgesehenen Pflichtinformationen (Art. 13, 14 DS-GVO) erweitert werden". — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 1.3.1](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- **Profiling limit:** "Eingriffsintensivere Maßnahmen wie automatisierte Selektionsverfahren zur Erstellung detaillierter Profile, Verhaltensprognosen bzw. Analysen, die zu zusätzlichen Erkenntnissen führen, sprechen hingegen dafür, dass das Interesse der betroffenen Person … überwiegt. In diesen Fällen handelt es sich um Profiling, das nicht mehr auf Art. 6 Abs. 1 UAbs. 1 lit. f DS-GVO gestützt werden kann und damit die Einholung einer ausdrücklichen Einwilligung … erforderlich macht. Der Verweis auf das Widerspruchsrecht des Art. 21 DS-GVO reicht nicht aus." By contrast, selection with no extra insight (e.g. by postcode) usually passes the balancing test. Profiles built from external sources such as social networks will usually fail it. — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 1.3.2 a/b](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- Art. 14 information for data not collected from the person: at the latest with the first communication, or within one month. If combined with the advertising, information and advertising text must be clearly separated, and the objection notice must stand out. The Art. 21(4) notice must be "deutlich abgesetzt" and not hidden in long texts, and the DSK recommends it "bei jeder Werbesendung". Information may be given through a URL or **QR code** (layered approach). — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 2.1, 2.2, 5.2](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- Repurposing data (e.g. data collected for another purpose now used for advertising) requires the Art. 6(4) compatibility test. — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 1.5](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- Personalised postal advertising based on legitimate interest was upheld by OLG Stuttgart. This was reported by a law-firm blog; details not verified. — [IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/olg-stuttgart-briefwerbung-datenschutz.html)

**§ 25 TDDDG (scan tracking)**
- § 25 Abs. 1 requires consent for storing information on, or reading information from, the user's device, regardless of whether the data is personal. The rule is technology-neutral: cookies, local and session storage, and fingerprinting (also when built server-side from browser data or read via JavaScript). — [DSK OH Digitale Dienste v1.2, Nov. 2024, Rn. 21–26](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH_Digitale_Dienste.pdf)
- Exception for an "ausdrücklich gewünschter digitaler Dienst": users request a service "indem sie ihn bewusst aufrufen … durch die Eingabe der URL … durch das Anklicken eines Links". This does not mean every function is wanted. Even simple visitor counting is "nicht per se als Bestandteil des Basisdienstes einzustufen". Counting via logfiles without personal data, or via a simple first-party counting pixel, is described as the original form of reach measurement. — [DSK OH Digitale Dienste Rn. 70, 88–90](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH_Digitale_Dienste.pdf)
- If § 25 consent is needed but missing, all later DSGVO processing that depends on it is unlawful too. — [DSK OH Digitale Dienste Rn. 97–98](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH_Digitale_Dienste.pdf)
- Contrary signal: a Bavarian authority (BayLDA) presentation lists "Pixel / Tracking-Links" and "Authentifizierungstoken" under the scope of § 25 TDDDG, and § 28 Abs. 1 Nr. 13 TDDDG makes violations a fine offence. — reported in [BIHK, "Webseiten datenschutzkonform gestalten", 28.07.2025](https://www.bihk.de/fileadmin/eigene_dateien/bihk/Teaserbilder/Sonstiges/Digitalisierung/20250728_Webseiten_datenschutzkonform_gestalten.pdf) (via search summary; slide not read in full)

**Retargeting / Custom Audiences**
- VG Bayreuth (08.05.2018, B 1 S 18.105, interim proceedings) upheld the BayLDA: uploading hashed customer e-mails to Facebook Custom Audiences without informed consent is unlawful, and Facebook is not a processor. Commentators say the result is unchanged under DSGVO Art. 6(1)(f). — [Dr. Datenschutz (blog)](https://www.dr-datenschutz.de/vg-bayreuth-bestaetigt-facebook-custom-audiences-ist-rechtswidrig/); [IHK Bayreuth: "Facebook Custom Audience nur mit Einwilligung"](https://www.ihk.de/bayreuth/hauptnavigation/service/recht/datenschutz/facebook-custom-audience-nur-mit-einwilligung-4439738); [BayLDA press release 2018](https://www.lda.bayern.de/media/pm2018_09.pdf). A VGH München decision (2018) reportedly followed; outcome not verified.
- Ad pixels or tags on the demo page (Meta Pixel, Google Ads remarketing) read from or write to the device, so they need § 25 consent and, under DSK Rn. 98, a lawful DSGVO basis for the downstream processing. — [DSK OH Digitale Dienste](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH_Digitale_Dienste.pdf)

**WhatsApp / SMS**
- "Elektronische Post" is to be read broadly and also covers SMS, Facebook and WhatsApp messages, so prior express consent is needed. — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- OLG Hamm (Hinweisbeschluss 17.05.2023, 18 U 154/22) treats "sämtliche Nachrichten über Social Media-Dienste wie Xing, Facebook, LinkedIn oder WhatsApp" and portal contact forms as electronic post requiring prior express consent (citing CJEU C-102/20 and OLG Nürnberg 15.01.2019, 3 U 724/18). An acquisition contract that required such contacts was void under § 134 BGB. The case concerned consumers. The blog author applies it to businesses as well. — [IT-Recht Kanzlei (law-firm blog)](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/olg-hamm-werbenachrichten-internetportale-social-media-einwilligung-erforderlich.html)

### Inferences
- **Scan logging (token → lead → timestamp) on our own server, with no cookies, fingerprinting or third-party scripts:** probably lawful under Art. 6(1)(f), provided the first letter's Art. 14 notice says that scans of the personal code are recorded, for what purpose and for how long. This fits the project's existing design (L4: "keine vollständige IP, keine Tracking-Cookies"). A residual risk remains because of the BayLDA "Tracking-Links" view. Mitigations: no cookies or local storage, no fingerprinting, no third-party embeds before consent, short retention, data minimisation (event plus date, not detailed click paths).
- **Detailed behaviour analysis per lead** (session replay, scroll depth, time on page tied to the lead, scoring "hot leads" from many signals) moves toward the profiling the DSK says cannot rest on Art. 6(1)(f). The project's follow-up rule 3 (≥ 2 demo visits or CTA click → "hot lead" letter) is a simple selection with a small but real behavioural element. In my view this is a **grey zone**: defensible if it is disclosed, minimal and objectable, but a regulator could call it profiling. Uncertain.
- **Retargeting ads after a scan: forbidden without consent** (cookie banner opt-in for pixels; list upload only with consent). Practically irrelevant without consent.
- **WhatsApp or SMS to the public number: forbidden.** A wa.me link where the business writes to **us** is fine, as already designed.

### Gaps
- No German authority statement or court decision found specifically on **personalised QR codes / PURLs in postal mail** under § 25 TDDDG. The BayLDA slide was only seen through a search summary.
- The VGH München outcome and any post-DSGVO court ruling on Custom Audiences were not verified.
- The OLG Stuttgart ruling on personalised postal advertising was not read in full (Az. not captured).

## 3. Lawful consent mechanisms on the demo page (double opt-in, "Rückruf erwünscht", visitor-started chat)

### Takeaway
The clean route is to let the visitor **ask** for contact: a callback form (express consent to one call about this offer), an e-mail reminder with double opt-in, or a chat or WhatsApp message they start. Each consent must be separate, specific to the channel and purpose, documented (the wording version plus a timestamp; for phone, § 7a UWG documentation and 5-year retention applies to **consumer** phone consent), and as easy to withdraw as to give. It must not be tied to unrelated things (Kopplungsverbot).

### Cited Findings
- Exception to the phone ban: "wenn die angesprochene Person selbst um den Anruf gebeten hat". A phone consent must be a separate declaration only about phone advertising. A consent bundled with other content is invalid (BGH, 14.04.2011, I ZR 30/10). Pre-formulated consents are valid only within the contract's purpose (BGH, 14.04.2011, I ZR 50/09). — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- § 7a UWG: express consent to **phone advertising to consumers** must be documented appropriately when given and kept for 5 years. The period restarts with every use, and records must be shown to the authority on request. Fines under § 20 UWG: up to €300,000 for unlawful consumer calls, up to €50,000 for documentation breaches. — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf); DSK also references Art. 7(1) DSGVO and § 7a UWG: [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 1.4.2 a](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- Double opt-in is "geboten" for electronic consents. The consent must be fully provable including its wording. Storing an IP address alone is not enough. A confirmation e-mail sent from the address (e.g. DKIM-signed) can prove e-mail consent. **A DOI e-mail does not prove consent for a phone number** entered on a website (BGH, 10.02.2011, I ZR 164/09). SMS or phone confirmation codes have little evidential value. A written consent is the best proof for phone numbers. — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 3.3](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf); [rewis.io I ZR 164/09](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/lul-09-02-2011-i-zr-16409/)
- Kopplungsverbot (Art. 7(4) DSGVO): freedom of consent is doubtful if a service depends on consent that is not needed for it. — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 3.4](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- Withdrawal must be possible at any time, informally, through any usual channel, and as easy as giving consent. Requiring a letter or fax, case numbers or a callback is not allowed. — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- Consent does not expire by time alone (BGH, 01.02.2018, III ZR 196/17), but under Art. 5 DSGVO long non-use may stop the advertiser relying on it. The EDPB recommends refreshing consent. — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 3.5](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- § 7 Abs. 3 UWG (existing customers) applies only once an address was obtained "im Zusammenhang mit dem Verkauf". A visitor who asked a question but did not buy is **not** an existing customer. — [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf)
- A one-off feedback or rating request is advertising (BGH VI ZR 225/17), so "Wie gefällt Ihnen der Entwurf?" e-mails also need consent. — [dejure.org](https://dejure.org/2018,28246)

### Inferences
- **"Rückruf erwünscht" form:** phone number, preferred time, an unticked checkbox or the submit button with clear text ("Ich möchte, dass [Absender] mich unter dieser Nummer einmalig zu meinem Website-Entwurf anruft. Widerruf jederzeit."), stored with a timestamp, the wording version and the slug. The § 7a 5-year retention strictly applies only to consumers, but keeping the same record for B2B is cheap and helps in court (burden of proof is on the advertiser). Risk: someone enters a third party's number. Plausibility checks help, for example comparing the number with the lead's known public number.
- **E-mail reminder ("Erinnern Sie mich vor Ablauf"):** a separate checkbox, double opt-in, the purpose limited to reminders and offers about this website draft, an unsubscribe link in every mail. The answer to the visitor's own question may be sent without marketing consent, but it should not contain additional advertising unless consent was given.
- **Chat or WhatsApp started by the visitor:** replying to the visitor's own question is allowed. Further unsolicited advertising messages in that thread are not, unless consent was given. Opt-in for later marketing messages has to be separate.
- No consent may be a condition for seeing the demo or buying (Kopplungsverbot). Consent checkboxes must not be pre-ticked (general DSGVO standard; not separately sourced here).

### Gaps
- No primary source fetched for the CJEU Planet49 holding on pre-ticked boxes (only quoted in the DSK OH). It is well known but not verified in this session.
- No source found on whether § 7a UWG documentation duties apply by analogy to B2B phone consent. The text and the IHK refer to consumers.

## 4. Court cases or IHK / consumer-body guidance on "personalisierte Landingpage / PURL" follow-ups

### Takeaway
I found **no German court decision and no IHK, Verbraucherzentrale or DSK guidance specifically on PURL or personalised QR landing-page follow-ups**. The assessment has to be built from the general rules above. The DSK does expressly allow privacy information to be delivered through a QR code or URL printed on advertising.

### Cited Findings
- The DSK allows layered information "beispielsweise durch eine URL, einen QR-Code oder das Angebot einer postalischen Zusendung". — [DSK OH Direktwerbung Nr. 2.1](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf)
- Searches for case law on PURL or QR tracking in advertising letters returned only general § 25 TDDDG and Briefwerbung material. — e.g. [IT-Recht Kanzlei on Briefwerbung](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/rechtliche-voraussetzungen-briefwerbung-verbraucher.html), [Dr. Datenschutz on Werbung per Post](https://www.dr-datenschutz.de/werbung-per-post-neukunden-dsgvo-konform-akquirieren/)

### Inferences
- The absence of case law is itself a risk signal. The first enforcement would likely come from a competitor's Abmahnung (UWG) or a data subject's complaint to the state authority (LDI NRW for Dortmund), not from a test case on PURLs.

### Gaps
- The DDV (Deutscher Dialogmarketing Verband) guidance and Verbraucherzentrale material were not reached in this session.
- No search on Austrian or other EU authority positions on PURLs.

## 5. Summary map: what the operator may do right after a scan (Handwerksbetrieb, no purchase, no contact consent)

### Takeaway
After a scan without contact consent, only **post** (and a visitor-initiated contact) is clearly safe. Phone is a high-risk grey zone and should be treated as off-limits. E-mail, SMS, WhatsApp, fax and retargeting are forbidden without express consent.

### Cited Findings
(Each row's basis is cited in Q1 to Q3. Key sources: [IHK Arnsberg](https://www.ihk-arnsberg.de/upload/Werbung_per_Telefon_Brief_E_mail_43871.pdf), [DSK OH Direktwerbung](https://www.datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH-Werbung_Februar%202022_final.pdf), [DSK OH Digitale Dienste](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH_Digitale_Dienste.pdf), [OLG Hamm via IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/olg-hamm-werbenachrichten-internetportale-social-media-einwilligung-erforderlich.html), [VG Bayreuth via IHK Bayreuth](https://www.ihk.de/bayreuth/hauptnavigation/service/recht/datenschutz/facebook-custom-audience-nur-mit-einwilligung-4439738).)

| Action after a scan | Status | Conditions / reason |
|---|---|---|
| Follow-up **postal letter** (incl. demo extension, "noch bis {Datum} online") | **Allowed** | No objection or opt-out (incl. "Kein Interesse" button), no known "Keine Werbung" sticker, recognisable as advertising, Art. 21 notice set apart in every letter, Art. 14 information given with the first letter |
| Letter that says "Sie haben Ihren Entwurf angesehen" | **Risky (DSGVO)** | Only if scan logging was disclosed in the Art. 14 notice. Keep it minimal. Detailed behaviour scoring may count as profiling that needs consent (DSK 1.3.2 b). A neutral wording is safer |
| Server-side scan logging via token (no cookies, no fingerprint, no third-party tools) | **Probably allowed** | Art. 6(1)(f), disclosed, short retention. Grey area because BayLDA lists "Tracking-Links" under § 25 TDDDG |
| Analytics, heatmaps or session replay on the demo tied to the lead | **Consent needed** | § 25 TDDDG for device access, plus the profiling concern |
| **Phone call** without the business asking for it | **High risk / avoid** | B2B presumed consent needs concrete prior facts showing interest. A scan after our unsolicited letter is weak, there is no case law in favour, and the BGH rejects "usefulness" and industry practice as reasons |
| Phone call after a **"Rückruf erwünscht"** request | **Allowed** | Express, separate, documented consent. Stay within the scope requested (this offer, one call) |
| **E-mail** to the publicly listed address | **Forbidden** | Prior express consent required for B2B too. One mail is enough to violate. § 7 Abs. 3 does not apply (not a customer) |
| E-mail reminder after **double opt-in** on the demo | **Allowed** | Separate checkbox, DOI, unsubscribe in every mail, documentation |
| **SMS / WhatsApp / Messenger / LinkedIn DM** to the business | **Forbidden** | Counts as "elektronische Post", needs prior express consent |
| Reply to a chat or WhatsApp the **visitor started** | **Allowed** | Answer the question. Further advertising messages only with a separate opt-in |
| **Fax** | **Forbidden** | Express consent required, also B2B; presumed consent does not count |
| **Retargeting** (Meta/Google Custom Audiences, ad pixels) | **Forbidden without consent** | List upload needs consent (VG Bayreuth). Pixels need § 25 consent |
| Visiting in person / door-to-door | Not researched | Outside scope; no sources gathered |

### Inferences
- The project's existing rules (letters only, `wa.me` link for inbound contact only, no cold mails or calls, an e-mail reminder only if the lead wrote to us) are consistent with this map. The two places to tighten are (1) the Art. 14 notice wording on scan recording and on using visit counts for follow-up selection, and (2) adding explicit opt-ins on the demo page (callback request, e-mail reminder with DOI) to create lawful channels.
- Fines under § 20 UWG target consumer calls. In B2B the practical risk is a competitor's or association's Abmahnung (costs plus a contractual penalty, usually several thousand euros per breach) and, under DSGVO, authority action or claims.

### Gaps
- Any 2025–2026 changes (e.g. ePrivacy reform, new BGH decisions on B2B presumed consent or on PURLs) were not found in these searches. A lawyer should confirm before relying on this.
